Kanazawa Castle Park — Part 2

The wind was still strong, and the snow kept blowing in at a forty-five-degree angle as I slowly walked in through the Kuro-mon entrance of Kanazawa Castle Park. The feeling in that moment was beyond what words could fully capture.

I was finally able to see the vast park spread out before me, covered in snow. As I took in the overwhelming beauty of the snowy landscape, a gentle warmth rose in my chest, and even in that stormy weather I genuinely felt glad I had come. A small smile naturally appeared on my face. That said, the wind was still fierce, and the cold was harsh. I was not wearing a raincoat, so I was just barely managing to hold my umbrella in place as I walked.

Carefully making my way along the slushy road, I noticed two signboards on my right. They were both made of dark brown wood, each surrounded by a low fence about knee height. On top of each small roof, a layer of white snow had settled, wrapping them in a quiet winter atmosphere.

I stepped closer to look at the signs beneath the roofs. The one on the left read “Kanazawa Castle & Kenrokuen Garden,” with a map showing the entire area and marking the current location. I realized that this was where I was. Satisfied with that, I turned my eyes to the sign on the right. It read “National Historic Site - Kanazawa Castle Ruins,” and displayed a map of Kanazawa Castle from the late Edo period along with a diagram based on historical site assessments. With that, I decided to keep moving forward.

The wide path had been cleared of snow, and I could tell it was well maintained. Even so, there were hardly any people around, and walking through this breathtaking snowy scenery felt incredibly pleasant. Even if the wind was strong and the snow kept falling. Still, I could not help but wonder how far I would end up walking from there, and a small sense of unease crept in. The place was simply vast. Yet, even as I carried that unease, the snowy landscape seemed to wash it away, and I continued along the broad walkway.

The sky was a whitish gray. Of course, it was completely overcast. On the right side of the path, a wooden fence about knee height ran along, clearly marking the route.

Here and there, I saw trees whose branches were bare, having shed their leaves for winter, blending into the cloudy sky, the snow-covered ground, and the muted background. That, in its own way, was beautiful and full of quiet charm. Under the pale gray sky, in a world of snow stretching out across the ground, stood zelkova trees with their winter branches exposed. The contrast felt like something out of a fairy tale, a strangely enchanting world.

As I kept walking, another map sign like the one from earlier appeared at my feet. This time, there was no roof—just the map itself placed there. Even so, the current location was clearly marked, which made it quite convenient. So this was where I was, I thought, and as I looked up, I noticed the Hishi Yagura turret peeking out above the stone walls. That area over there must be what people thought of as the central part of Kanazawa Castle.

With that thought in mind, I continued to walk on. I was finally heading toward Ninomaru Square, where Kanazawa Castle stands. For now, I decided to end this part of the walk here.

Even in this bad weather, I had walked this far with a strange sense of having forgotten about time altogether. I will probably end up with sore muscles a few days from now.

金沢城公園 ― Part 2

相変わらず強風と斜め四十五度の積雪の中、金沢城公園の黒門口からゆっくりと歩いていく。 その時の感動と言ったら、言葉では表しきれない。

壮大な公園に広がる雪景色を見ることができたのだ。 圧巻の雪景色にじんわりと胸が温かくなり、強風と積雪の中でも来てよかったと心底思い、ふと笑みがこぼれる。 とはいえ、風は相変わらず強く、寒さも厳しい。 レインコートではなかったので、傘をかろうじて支えながら歩いていた。

みぞれ状になった道路に気をつけながら進んでいくと、右側に二つの看板が目に入る。 全体がダークブラウンの木製で、一つ一つにひざ下ほどの柵が囲ってある。 それぞれの屋根の上には白い雪が積もり、冬の静けさをまとっていた。

屋根の下にある看板を見てみる。 左側には「金沢城・兼六園」と書かれ、その下に現在地を示す全体の地図が描かれている。 なるほど、今はこのあたりかと納得しながら、右側の看板に目を移す。 そこには「国指定史跡　金沢城跡」と書かれ、江戸時代後期の金沢城絵図と史蹟鑑定に基づく図面が掲げられている。 ともかく、歩を進めることにした。

広い歩道は除雪され、手入れの行き届いた様子がうかがえる。 それにしても、ほとんど人がいない中で、この絶景の雪景色を歩くのは気分がいい。 例え強風と積雪の中だったとしても。 これから一体どれくらい歩くのだろうかと少し不安にもなる。 ともかく広い。 そんな不安を抱えながらも、それらを打ち消すかのような雪景色の中、広い歩道を進んでいった。

空の色はグレーがかった白。 もちろん曇り空だ。 歩道の右側には木製のひざ下ほどの柵が続き、順路が分かりやすい。

ところどころに見える 冬木立 は、曇り空と地面の雪と背景に溶け込んでいる。 それはそれで風情があり、美しい。 薄いグレーの空と曇りの下で、一面に広がる雪景色の地面に佇む、冬木立のケヤキ。 そのコントラストは、おとぎ話にも出てきそうな、なんとも幻想的な世界のようだった。

歩いていくと、再びさきほどの地図の案内板が足元に現れた。 今回は屋根はなく、地図だけが置かれている。 現在地が分かりやすく書かれているので、これはなかなか便利だ。 なるほど、今はこの地点かと確認しながらふと見上げると、城壁の上に菱櫓がひょこっと姿を見せていた。 あのあたりが、いわゆる金沢城の中心部なのだろう。

そんなことを考えながら、さらに歩を進めていく。 いよいよ金沢城のある二の丸広場方面へ向かっていくのだ。 今回はここまでにする。

それにしても、悪天候の中、時間を忘れてしまうような感覚でここまで歩いてきた。 きっと数日後には筋肉痛になるのだろう。
