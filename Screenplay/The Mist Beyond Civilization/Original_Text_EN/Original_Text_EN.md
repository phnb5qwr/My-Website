The Mist Beyond Civilization
A Record by AER

AER was climbing a mountain.
Together with dozens of other people.
They might have been explorers.
They might have been journalists.
They might have been scholars.
Or perhaps they were simply a group of people who believed themselves to be at the forefront of the world.
________________________________________
AER was not entirely sure.
But there was one thing he understood.
They were chasing a legend.
A city said to exist beyond the mist.
A city no one had ever entered.
A city whose truth no one knew.
That was the place they sought.
________________________________________
AER felt slightly troubled.
Because he knew that place.
________________________________________
When they reached near the summit, the mist appeared.
A massive wall of white stretching across the mountains.
The expedition members became excited.
Some raised their cameras.
Some deployed observation devices.
Others began arguing theories.
Everyone seemed determined to prove something.
AER, however, was different.
He watched the scene the way one watches something strangely familiar.
Like an old memory returning.
________________________________________
Beyond the mist stood a town.
Yet it was surprisingly ordinary.
A rural town.
Quiet neighborhoods.
Small parks.
Old local shops.
The kind of scenery one might find in a travel brochure.
The explorers seemed confused.
They had expected something far greater.
________________________________________
But AER knew.
That was not its true form.
________________________________________
The town was protected by a perception shield.
Not a weapon.
Not a defensive barrier.
It was a shield that altered perception itself.
It changed what people saw according to their desires.
Those who sought power saw only a rural town.
Those who wished to obtain advanced technology saw only a rural town.
Those who chased status and influence saw only a rural town.
And so none of them could discover the truth.
________________________________________
The real town was entirely different.
Countless vehicles moved through the skies.
Yet nobody paid attention.
In AER's world, such vehicles were no more remarkable than public rental bicycles.
________________________________________
Transformation technology existed as well.
It was part of everyday life.
At dinner, someone might casually say,
"I think I'll eat tonight as a giant monster."
And nobody would find that unusual.
________________________________________
People could change their appearance freely.
They could become old.
They could become young.
They could appear male.
They could appear female.
They could take on any ethnicity.
Physical appearance carried little significance in a civilization that had long moved beyond such things.
________________________________________
Money no longer existed.
Energy was freely available.
Medical care was freely available.
Food shortages no longer existed.
Governmental systems were jointly managed by humans and humanoids.
It was a society built through tens of thousands of years of trial and error.
________________________________________
Yet what AER found most unusual was not the technology.
It was the fact that nobody boasted about it.
Because it was ordinary.
________________________________________
Just as people on Earth do not boast about breathing.
Just as they do not boast about running water.
Advanced technology had simply become part of everyday life.
________________________________________
Meanwhile, the expedition was thrilled.
They believed they had discovered something.
They believed they had uncovered one of the world's greatest secrets.
Yet from AER's perspective,
the situation was slightly different.
________________________________________
They were tourists.
________________________________________
He did not look down on them.
He did not view them as enemies.
They were simply tourists.
Visitors who would see what was visible to them,
and then return home.
Nothing more.
________________________________________
What made the situation even more amusing was that researchers existed in AER's homeland as well.
Except they studied Earth.
In their libraries were books such as:
The Curious Habits of Earth Humans
Currency: The Illusionary Game
Politics as an Ancient System
Why Humans Argue on Social Media Every Day
And many others.
________________________________________
Yet none of these were considered important academic works.
They were travel journals.
Magazines.
Columns.
Sometimes even comedy books.
________________________________________
One day,
AER decided to write a book about his experience.
The title was:
The Man Excluded from the Super Civilization Exploration Tour
________________________________________
The book was placed in the library of his homeland.
Not in the academic section.
Not in the science fiction section.
________________________________________
It was placed in the section reserved for absurd curiosities.
________________________________________
Readers laughed.
"Oh yes, I know that place."
"Earth really was like that."
"This was from the era when people still used money, wasn't it?"
"How nostalgic."
________________________________________
And AER quietly drank his tea while listening to them.
In the rural town beyond the mist.
In a forgotten corner of a civilization invisible to the outside world.
Simply sitting there.
Quietly.
