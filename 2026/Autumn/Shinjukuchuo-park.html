Shinjuku Chuo Park Several days of rain had lingered without end. Yet on that particular day, the sky was remarkably clear. Blue skies stretched overhead, while white clouds drifted slowly across the horizon. It was a refreshing early autumn morning. A short walk from the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building lies Shinjuku Chuo Park. Having learned that Kumano Shrine was located nearby, I decided to pay a visit. Although it was already early autumn, the lingering warmth of late summer could still be felt. Perhaps it would become hot after walking for a while. Leaving the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building No. 1, I crossed a bridge toward the park. Only later did I learn that this bridge was called Rainbow Bridge. It was around midday, yet there were not many people around. Under the blue autumn sky, I continued walking at an unhurried pace. After crossing the bridge, a park information map came into view. Drawing closer, I found the current location marker. It appeared that Kumano Shrine was situated diagonally across the park. However, with such pleasant weather overhead, it felt natural to take my time and wander through the grounds. I headed toward the shaded pathway lined with trees. As I walked beneath the greenery, the distant sound of flowing water gradually emerged. A steady rush echoed through the quiet air. Soon I arrived at a water plaza known as "Fun More Time Plaza." The name reminded me of the map I had seen earlier. Seeing it once again brought a faint smile. Beyond the plaza, true to its name, water flowed continuously. Rows of light-brown bricks stretched across the scene, while walls of varying heights rose in gentle tiers behind them. Water descended softly from the upper sections of each level. In front of the fountain stood piles of carefully stacked stones. For a moment, I wondered about them. Then, after quietly taking in the view from the walkway, I continued on my way. Following the shaded path farther into the park, the sound of running water returned once more. Before long, a long waterfall appeared along the right side of the path. It was known as the "Shinjuku Niagara Falls." Water streamed continuously down a long brown wall. The name immediately brought the park map back to mind. So this was the waterfall I had seen marked there. At the same time, the slightly humorous name brought another quiet smile. Yet the view itself was surprisingly pleasant. Standing there for a while, I listened to the sound of the water and enjoyed the scene before moving on. Farther ahead, an open grassy area came into view. There, rows of miniature sunflowers appeared to be in bloom. Beneath the clear early autumn sky, the small blossoms swayed gently in the breeze. Their bright yellow petals quietly colored the path beside passing visitors.
